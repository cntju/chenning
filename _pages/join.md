## Who should get in touch?

I welcome enquiries from motivated students and researchers with interests in **computer networks, Internet of Things, edge computing, AI/ML, graph learning, optimization, distributed systems, or related areas**. Strong applicants do not need to have worked on exactly the same topics before; curiosity, technical foundations and the ability to formulate research questions matter more.

## Research topics you may work on

1. **Resilient IoT / IIoT** — topology repair, robustness enhancement, failure recovery, service continuity.
2. **AI for Networking** — learning-assisted network control, graph learning, multi-agent decision-making, agentic networking.
3. **Edge Intelligence** — edge server placement, heterogeneous QoS, workload-aware resource allocation.
4. **Autonomous Network Self-Healing** — online sensing, retrieval, coordination, safe topology actions.
5. **Complex & Computing-Power Networks** — cascading failures, capacity-aware dynamics, black-swan vulnerabilities and path enhancement.

## Before you email

Please include the following so I can understand your fit quickly:

- **CV** — education, research experience, technical skills and publications/projects.
- **Transcript** — especially for prospective degree students.
- **Research interests** — 3–6 sentences explaining what problems you want to study.
- **Representative work** — paper, thesis, GitHub repository, technical report, competition project, or other evidence of research/engineering ability.
- **Your plan** — tell me whether you are looking for a PhD, MSc project, RA opportunity, research visit, or collaboration, and your preferred start period.

## A good first email

Keep it concise. A useful subject line is:

`Prospective PhD/MSc/RA – [Your Name] – [Research Topic]`

In the body, introduce yourself, explain **why your interests connect with my research**, mention one or two relevant experiences, and attach your CV. Generic mass emails are much harder to evaluate than a short message that demonstrates genuine research fit.

## What I value in researchers

I value people who are curious, reliable and willing to understand the underlying problem rather than only apply a fashionable method. Good research often requires a combination of **clear problem formulation, careful experiments, technical depth, and strong communication**.
